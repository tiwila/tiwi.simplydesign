## The Problem

The goal wasn’t just a new look, but a better experience. The initial redesign direction improved visual polish, but internal teams still struggled to update seasonal content without developer support.

At the same time, recruiting goals required better analytics and SEO control than the no-code delivery approach could support. We had a UX direction, but not a sustainable operational model.

## The Why Moment

The pivot happened when I stopped asking, “Which tool is best?” and started asking, “Which sequence protects both usability and operations?” That reframed the work into three phases: strategic UX redesign, an editable handoff, and a production-ready migration.

## What I Did

### Phase 01: Strategic UX Redesign (Figma)
I performed a heuristic evaluation of the legacy site to identify what worked and what didn’t. By keeping the familiar structural layout that existing users relied on, I focused on UX best practices: tightening information architecture, improving button accessibility, and building a clearer visual hierarchy.

### Phase 02: Handoff & Accessibility (Canva)
To prioritize the internal team’s day-to-day workflow, I rebuilt the site using Canva’s website feature. This empowered non-technical staff to make real-time updates to banners and text while keeping the redesigned structure intact.

### Phase 03: The Code-Based Pivot (Lovable)
Refusing to compromise on performance, I used Lovable to migrate the design into a production-ready environment. This allowed me to export clean React + Tailwind CSS, integrate Google Tag Manager, and improve SEO — ensuring the design was not only beautiful, but also measurable and scalable.

## What Broke

I underestimated how quickly measurement and metadata requirements would become non-negotiable. The no-code phase solved immediate editing needs, but the lack of SEO and tracking control meant we had to pivot earlier than planned. That created rework, but prevented a weaker long-term launch.

## The Outcome

The final direction balanced user clarity with operational reality: a better experience for users, an editable workflow for internal teams, and a code-based foundation that supported SEO and analytics.

This project was a lesson in adaptability: the best tool isn’t always the one with the most features — it’s the one that best supports the real goal (in this case, balancing ease of updates with data-driven performance).
