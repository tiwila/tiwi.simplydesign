## The Problem

The designathon started with a strong visual identity but an unclear product story. Early reviews showed that people could describe the brand mood, but not what the product actually did or why it mattered. In internal critique notes, 6 out of 8 reviewers called the direction "visually interesting but functionally vague."

When I tested the first frames with peers, most feedback landed on the same point: "I can see the style, but I can't see the job this product does."

## The Why Moment

Three review sessions in, I noticed every critique was about layout and style, not comprehension. The issue was not that the UI looked unfinished. The issue was that we had not defined the core decision the interface needed to support. I paused all high-fidelity exploration, mapped the first-use journey, and used the logo and brand primitives as system constraints. That pivot gave every screen a clearer role and turned aesthetics into structure.

## What I Did

### Reframing the brief
Instead of polishing more screens, I reframed the objective around one question: what does MOSM help a user decide in their first interaction? I aligned the team on that core job before moving forward with additional UI detail.

### Building a system before pages
I translated brand rules into practical interface foundations: typographic hierarchy, color roles, and spacing rhythm. This reduced visual inconsistency and made later screen decisions faster and easier to defend.

### Validating comprehension early
I ran lightweight comprehension checks with peers at each iteration. That gave immediate signals about clarity and let me remove decorative patterns that looked good but did not improve understanding.

## What Broke

I underestimated how quickly visual momentum can hide strategic gaps. I spent too long refining screen polish before validating the product story, and that delayed meaningful feedback by almost a week. I corrected by resetting the sequence: define the decision first, then design the interface around it.

## The Outcome

MOSM moved from an identity-first concept to a direction with clearer product logic and stronger design-system discipline. The team left the designathon with a usable foundation, not just attractive frames, and we had a sharper path for phase-two validation.
